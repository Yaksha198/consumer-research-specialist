# Topic 3: Meal / gut-content weight

## Plain-language answer
Any food you have eaten but not yet passed adds to what the scale shows. How much depends on where the food is. A standard small test meal mostly leaves the stomach within about 4 hours, and fluids are absorbed fast, so by the next morning very little of last night's dinner is still in the stomach. What remains is the colon's standing content. MRI puts it at about 0.5-0.6 kg in healthy adults (roughly 480 mL on a low-fibre diet and 620 mL on a high-fibre diet). About a third of that turns over each day through meals and bowel movements. Adults in Western countries pass roughly 100-130 g of stool a day on average. So the day-to-day scale swing that comes purely from "food in transit" is on the order of **0.1-0.4 kg**. It can reach about 0.5 kg or more after a large late meal, a skipped bowel movement, or both. That is real, but it is smaller than the swings from water and glycogen. A low-carb, low-fibre diet lowers stool mass and the standing colonic content. It also makes constipation more common, which makes the day-to-day number noisier.

## Insulin resistance / population caveat
- Every transit and colon-volume figure above comes from **healthy, mostly normal-weight volunteers**. None comes from insulin-resistant adults with Class II obesity. I could not check whether colon volume scales with body size.
- The Abell reference meal is **255 kcal and low-fat**. A 700-1000 kcal high-protein, high-fat dinner empties more slowly, so the 4-hour retention figure understates how long it stays in the stomach. This is a general physiological principle that I did not check against a specific source in this session. Even so, a 10-12 hour overnight gap is almost certainly enough for the stomach to empty [likely].
- Diabetic hyperglycaemia is known to slow gastric emptying. The subject has IR but not diagnosed diabetes. The data on gastric emptying in obesity alone is inconsistent, and I did not verify it here.
- The **low-carb evidence does come from obese populations**. Brinkworth 2009 studied adults with abdominal obesity (BMI 26-44) and found that the very-low-carb diet lowered faecal output and defecation frequency. Yancy 2004 found much more constipation on a ketogenic diet. So for this subject, expect a **smaller standing stool mass** but **more irregular emptying**.

### 1. Abell 2008 gastric emptying consensus
- **Citation:** Abell TL, Camilleri M, Donohoe K, et al. 2008. Consensus recommendations for gastric emptying scintigraphy: a joint report of the American Neurogastroenterology and Motility Society and the Society of Nuclear Medicine. *Am J Gastroenterol* (March 2008). PMID 18287197. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18287197/ (volume/pages not confirmed; also reprinted in *J Nucl Med Technol* 36(1):44)
- **Population:** reference ranges from healthy adults. **Mismatch vs subject:** healthy, normal-weight; the test meal is far smaller than a real dinner.
- **Finding / number:** retention 37-90% at 1 h, 30-60% at 2 h, 0-10% at 4 h. Retention above 10% at 4 h counts as delayed emptying.
- **Quality / limitations:** standing clinical standard; only a small, low-fat meal was tested; the ranges quoted come from a secondary snippet.

### 2. Bendezú 2017 colonic content (Azpiroz group, MRI)
- **Citation:** Bendezú RA, Mego M, Monclus E, Merino X, Accarino A, Malagelada JR, Navazo I, Azpiroz F. 2017. Colonic content: effect of diet, meals, and defecation. *Neurogastroenterol Motil* 29(2):e12930. DOI 10.1111/nmo.12930; PMID 27545449. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27545449/ (abstract only)
- **Population:** 10 healthy subjects (age/sex/BMI not confirmed). **Mismatch vs subject:** very small, healthy group; the diets lasted only 3 days.
- **Finding / number:** low- vs high-residue diet: colonic content 479 vs 616 mL; faecal output 145 vs 223 mL/day; right-colon content +41 mL 4 h after a meal; about 1/3 of content turns over daily.
- **Quality / limitations:** the most direct measurement of "mass in transit", but n=10.

### 3. Rose 2015 faeces/urine review, and Cummings 1992
- **Citation:** Rose C, Parker A, Jefferson B, Cartmell E. 2015. The characterization of feces and urine: a review of the literature to inform advanced treatment technology. *Crit Rev Environ Sci Technol* 45(17):1827-1879. DOI 10.1080/10643389.2014.1000761; PMID 26246784; PMC4500995. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4500995/
- **Supporting:** Cummings JH, Bingham SA, Heaton KW, Eastwood MA. 1992. Fecal weight, colon cancer risk, and dietary intake of nonstarch polysaccharides (dietary fiber). *Gastroenterology* 103(6):1783-1789. PMID 1333426. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1333426/ (abstract only)
- **Population:** Rose pools 116 study means from healthy individuals worldwide. Cummings measured 220 healthy UK adults. **Mismatch vs subject:** mixed-diet populations, not low-carb and not obese.
- **Finding / number:** Rose: median 128 g/day wet (29 g dry); fibre intake is the main driver; about 2x higher in high-fibre populations. Cummings: UK median 106 g/day; population means 72-470 g/day.
- **Quality / limitations:** large pooled datasets, but between-study methods vary.

### 4. Wang 2015 wireless motility capsule normal values
- **Citation:** Wang YT, et al. 2015. Regional gastrointestinal transit and pH studied in 215 healthy volunteers using the wireless motility capsule: influence of age, gender, study country and testing protocol. *Aliment Pharmacol Ther* 42:761-772. DOI 10.1111/apt.13329; PMID 26223837. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26223837/ (abstract only)
- **Population:** 215 healthy volunteers across several countries. **Mismatch vs subject:** healthy; not obese.
- **Finding / number:** normal ranges of 2-5 h gastric, 2-6 h small bowel, 10-59 h colon, 10-73 h whole gut. Women had longer whole-gut transit by a median of 263 min. The ranges may come from a secondary review quoting this dataset.
- **Quality / limitations:** the capsule is indigestible and leaves the stomach with the fasting motility cycle, so its gastric time is not the same as meal emptying.

## Disagreements between sources
- **Colon content, standing:** Pritchard's fasting segment volumes add up to about 560 mL of total volume, which includes gas. Bendezú measured 480-620 mL of **non-gaseous** content. The two broadly agree, and both show that diet shifts the figure by 100-150 mL.
- **Stool mass:** Cummings (106 g/day, UK), Rose (128 g/day, pooled) and Bendezú (145-223 mL/day, controlled diets) differ mainly because of fibre intake and method. They agree on a range of about 100-220 g/day.
- **Transit:** whole-gut transit runs from 10 to 73 h (capsule), and Degen shows large test-retest variability within the same person. The "1-3 days" figure is defensible, but for any one person it is imprecise.
- **Coach's "50-60% retention at 1 h":** this sits inside the normal band (37-90%), but the band is wide. The 60% figure is the *2-hour* upper limit.

## Applying it to the subject
Worked estimate for the subject weighing himself at 07:00 after a dinner at 20:00-21:00. Assume about 800 kcal of high-protein, low-carb food (~600 g) plus ~700 mL of fluid:
- **Stomach at 07:00:** 10-11 h later, essentially empty. Allow 0-50 g for a large, fatty meal [likely].
- **Fluid:** absorbed within hours. That mass now counts as body water or urine, **not gut content** (see the water topic).
- **Dinner residue in the small bowel and colon:** the undigested part of a low-fibre meal is small. Right-colon content rises about 40 mL within 4 h of a meal (Bendezú). The added in-transit mass from last night's dinner is probably **~0.1-0.3 kg** [guessing].
- **Standing colonic content:** about 0.5 kg, which is always there and not "extra". On his low-fibre diet it is probably near the low-residue value (~480 mL), so **~0.1-0.15 kg lower** than on a higher-fibre diet [likely]. That permanent reduction is a small part (≤0.2 kg) of his early reported loss, whether that loss is 9 kg or the ~6 kg his BMI change implies.
- **Bowel-movement timing:** a single missed movement keeps about one day's stool (~100-150 g) on board. Constipation on low-carb could stack 2-3 days' worth, **~0.3-0.5 kg** [likely].
- **Net:** morning-to-morning noise from gut contents alone is **about ±0.1-0.4 kg**, with occasional extremes of ~0.5-0.7 kg. For a ~100-107 kg man, the weekly weight cycle of ~0.35% (~0.35-0.4 kg) is of similar size and includes water. Gut contents cannot explain multi-kilogram changes.

Confidence: medium, because the gastric, stool and colonic-content anchors agree across independent sources, but all data come from healthy and mostly lean volunteers, Bendezú is n=10, the "dinner residue" figure is my own inference, and no source page could be fetched directly for verification.
