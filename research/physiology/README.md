## Issues that affect several files

- **The inputs contradict each other.** BMI 35 → 33 at a typical height is about 6 kg of loss, not the reported 9 kg. Topics 1, 5 and 6 compute both cases. Resolve this with actual height and weigh-ins before relying on any per-kg estimate.
- **The protein threshold in the brief doesn't match the subject.** 150–180 g/day at ~105–110 kg is under 2 g/kg of actual body weight (topic 4).
- **Ketosis can't be ruled out.** At 30–60 g carbs/day it confounds both the appetite question (topic 4) and the early "lean loss" readings (topic 5).
- **Most trial populations don't match him.** They are mostly lean young men (topics 1–3), or adults around 65–70 on higher-carb diets (topic 7).

## Combined estimate: a 1–1.5 kg overnight jump after a carb and salt refeed

Glycogen water 0.4–1.0 kg (topic 1), sodium water 0.2–0.6 kg (topic 2), gut contents 0.1–0.4 kg (topic 3). These overlap: insulin drives both glycogen storage and sodium retention. A 1–1.5 kg overnight jump is therefore fully explained without any fat gain [likely]. Fat gain can't physically be that fast: 1 kg of fat is about 7,000+ kcal above maintenance.
